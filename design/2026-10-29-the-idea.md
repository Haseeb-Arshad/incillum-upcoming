# Incillum: the idea

Written 29 October 2026, for the founder's funding application and as the
reasoning behind the front page and the private brief. The figures are sourced
in `design/2026-10-29-research.md`; what is built and what is not is
`requirements/README.md`.

---

## In one line

**Incillum is intelligence for commercial work.** Its agent, Charlie, picks up
a customer's request for quotation the moment it lands, reads every document
behind it, prices each line against real costs and the agreed margin, and hands
a person a decision instead of a backlog.

## The problem, as it feels

A buyer sends a request for quotation. It is an email with a PDF, a drawing
pack, a spreadsheet of twenty lines and a covering note that quietly changes
one of them. Somebody has to match every line to a product, find the current
cost in a supplier price list, apply the terms of the framework agreement, and
check the result against the margin floor. The documents never meet, so a
person carries the figures between them by hand.

That person is usually busy, often away, and never available at the moment the
request lands. Meanwhile the buyer has sent the same request to two other
suppliers. The quote that goes out a day later is either a little late, or a
little wrong, and nobody finds out which until the order goes elsewhere or the
margin is gone.

Nobody decided to keep that buyer waiting. The work simply had nobody attached
to it.

## The problem, in numbers

All third-party, all checked; see the research report for method and caveats.

- **42 hours**: the average reply to a web sales enquiry among 2,241 US
  companies that replied within a month. **23%** never replied at all.
  (Harvard Business Review, 2011)
- **Nearly 7×**: the odds of reaching a decision maker when the reply comes
  within the hour rather than an hour later; **more than 60×** against waiting
  a day. (Same)
- **17%** of a sales rep's week goes on creating quotes and **13%** on typing
  data in by hand, against **22%** with customers. (Salesforce, State of Sales,
  seventh edition, 4,050 professionals, 2025)
- **8%**: the rise in operating profit from a price 1% higher at the same
  volume, for the average S&P 1500 company. (McKinsey Quarterly, 2003)

Speed decides who gets the conversation. Margin decides whether it was worth
having. Both depend, today, on someone being at a desk with six documents open.

## The insight

The quotation is the most valuable document a commercial team produces and the
least automated. CPQ software makes the quote document once somebody knows what
to put in it; it does not do the reading, matching, costing and checking that
come first, which is where the hours and the margin go.

Language models can now read those documents. What they cannot be trusted to do
is decide a price. Incillum is built on that split: the model reads, code does
the arithmetic, and a person makes every decision that commits money or leaves
the building.

## The product

Charlie is one agent on every request, from the moment it lands to the moment a
person decides.

| It | What that means |
| --- | --- |
| Reads | Email, PDFs, drawings and spreadsheets, into lines: part, quantity, date, terms, each value tied to the page and passage it came from |
| Matches | Every line to a product the business carries; a line the evidence cannot settle is held with one question, never guessed |
| Prices | Costs from price lists and supplier replies, terms from the agreement, arithmetic in whole cents by code |
| Checks | The margin floor on every line, and the shortfall named when a line falls under it |
| Hands back | A quotation ready for a decision, with the working attached; nothing sent and no price committed until a person approves |

### Where it stops

Four limits, stated in advance and never argued down: **missing evidence**
(a figure with no source is reported missing, never estimated), **ambiguity**
(the line waits for a person), **commercial policy** (floors and authority are
checked on every line), and **approval** (nothing outward happens without a
person).

### Built to be checked

Every figure cited to its source; margins and totals in integer minor units by
one financial kernel; one approval gate in front of every outward action; a
record the application cannot rewrite; row-level security on every tenant
table; credentials sealed with AES-256-GCM. This is the answer to the question
every buyer and investor asks of an agent: what happens when it is wrong.

## The wedge

The first job is **RFQ to commercial decision**, for businesses that quote for
a living: manufacturers and distributors with parts to match, drawings to read,
suppliers whose prices move, and a margin someone signs off.

Why this job first:

- it is frequent, so the value compounds weekly, not yearly;
- it is document-heavy, which is exactly what models are now good at reading;
- it has a hard, checkable outcome: the hours a reply waited and the margin
  held on the lines it priced;
- it sits upstream of everything else (orders, follow-ups, documents, the
  financial consequences), so the same agent extends along the arc.

## Why now

Gartner expects the share of enterprise software applications that include
agentic AI to go from under 1% in 2024 to 33% by 2028. It also expects more than
40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027, over cost,
unclear value or weak risk controls.

Both are the opportunity. Agents are arriving in commercial work either way;
the ones that last will be the ones a finance director can audit. Incillum is
built that way from the first line, rather than retrofitting controls onto a
demo.

## The market

Configure, price and quote software alone is estimated at USD 2.8 to 3.5
billion in 2025, growing 9 to 17% a year (paid reports, wide range). That is the
tool, and a floor. The larger prize is the labour around it: 17% of every sales
rep's week, and the margin decided inside every quote.

## Where it stands

Pre-launch. Built and tested locally; no hosted environment yet; no customers.

- **Built locally:** reading requests into lines (customer uploads blocked
  until reader isolation is qualified), citations in the review, held lines,
  margin arithmetic, supplier comparison, the quotation and workbook documents,
  the approval gate.
- **Partly built:** citations carried into the quotation draft; repricing when
  a supplier answer reopens an approved price.
- **Not built:** asking suppliers, posting to a team, calls, sending to the
  customer (each will be an action behind the same gate), and the hosted
  environment.

The site describes the product in the present tense by the founder's decision;
`requirements/README.md` keeps the difference, line by line, for exactly the
conversation an investor will want to have.

## Go to market

Early access is one real case, run beside a team's own process, with nothing to
pay and nothing to change. The comparison is the buyer's own: how long the reply
took, and what margin it held. Design partners first; the waitlist on the site
is the front door.

## Risks, and the answers

| Risk | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| A wrong number is worse than a late one | Cited figures, held lines, arithmetic in code, a person approving anything outward |
| Every team's systems differ | Connections set up per team, reading first; nothing promised before it works |
| Agent washing makes buyers sceptical | A narrow job with outcomes the buyer measures themselves |
| No customers yet | Design partners through early access, one real case each |
| Model cost and reliability | The model only reads; pricing and checks are deterministic |

## Open questions for the founder

These are deliberately not answered here, because only the founder can:
the round size and use of funds, the pricing model (per workspace, per seat,
per quote handled), and the first ten design partners by name.

The page has one job: make a stranger who quotes for a living think "we need
this" before they decide whether to leave an address. Everything that did not
serve that was cut.

### What was removed, and why

- **The Charlie act, the six sources and the four tabs**, which each told the
  film's steps again. They are kept in the code, unplaced, in case one is
  wanted back.
- **The six steps under the Charlie page's film**, each with a still: the
  film already says them, chapter by chapter. The page now says something the
  film cannot: Charlie is talked to in a team's channel, it talks to the team,
  the suppliers and the telephone (each behind a person's approval), and it has
  limits (what it does alone, what waits for a person, what it never does).
- **The long case** of five sections with notes and sources: it read as a
  document. It became the private brief.
- **The blur-on-scroll**, which felt like an effect rather than a reason to
  look. Motion came back as scenes instead: each one shows something happening
  to the work, plays once, and is still for anyone who asks for less motion.
- **The night everywhere.** Speed is the message; the night is kept for the
  close.
- **Every em dash** a reader could see, and the dash in the page titles, which
  now use a straight line: *Incillum | Intelligence for commercial work*.

### Rules the words follow

Short sentences; the figures do the shouting. No dash in place of a comma. No
slogans in three beats. Nothing a person could not say across a desk. British
spelling. No figure about Incillum itself until there is one to print.

## The private brief

`/brief` is a password-protected page for investors and partners: the case in
full, what is built and what is not, the risks, and every source. The words
live only on the server and are sent to a browser only after the password is
given; the page is marked `noindex` and served `private, no-store`.

To share it, set `BRIEF_PASSWORD` (at least eight characters) in the hosting
environment's server variables, never with a `VITE_` prefix. Changing the
password signs everybody out.
